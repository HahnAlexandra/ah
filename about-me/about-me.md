Hi, I am Alex! An evolutionary biologist with a passion for creating art.

After graduating from high school, I did a year of ecological voluntary service (FÖJ) at the UNESCO World Heritage Wadden Sea. Afterwards, I completed my BSc in Bio Sciences at the University of Rostock and my MSc in Biological Oceanography at Kiel University and GEOMAR. I stayed at GEOMAR for my PhD working on salinity adaptation in the copepod *Acartia tonsa*. During my time as doctoral candidate I served for a 2-year term as Young Ambassador for the European Marine Board.

Throughout my career, I have always tried to find ways to combine art with science.

# FÖJ
During my Ecological Voluntary Service at the Naturschutzgemeinschaft Sylt e.V., I was part of a team dedicated to nature conservation and science communication on the island of Sylt. As part of my job, I guided tours through the UNESCO World Heritage Site Wadden Sea and two additional nature conservation areas managed by the organisation. I conducted scientific bird counts to monitor the populations of waterfowl. 

# Bachelor of Science
I completed my bachelor in Bio Sciences at the University of Rostock. During this time, I worked as a student assistant at the Marine Science Center Warnemünde, where I gave short lectures about ongoing research and animal care and was responsible for visitor support. I earned a DAAD stipend to spend one semester abroad at the Memorial University of Newfoundland and Labrador. In my final thesis, I investigated the population dynamics of *Arctica islandica* by analysing time-series data.

# Master of Science
I obtained my master in Biological Oceanography from Kiel University in a joint program with GEOMAR Helmholz Centre of Ocean Research Kiel. Part of my studies included a 1-month internship at the Ocean Sciences Centre in Logy Bay, NL and a student research cruise on the RV Elisabeth-Mann-Borgese. During my master, I worked as a research assistant at GEOMAR identifying and quantifying Baltic zooplankton as part of a long-term monitoring project. In my final thesis, I investigated the effect of phenotypic plasticity on seasonal thermal tolerance in the copepod *Acartia hudsonica*.

# EMB Young Ambassador
I was fortunate to be selected for a two-year term as Young Ambassador for the [European Marine Board](https://www.marineboard.eu/).
During this time, I represented Early Career Ocean Professionals (ECOPs) from 38 member organisations in plenary sessions and learned about the science-policy interface. I organised regular webinars and created social media content to increase ECOP visibility. The highlight of my work as Young Ambassador was the workshop on science-policy for ECOPs from member organisations that I organised with a small team of  Young Ambassadors after which we had the opportunity to present the outcomes of brainstorming session in a keynote speech during the EurOcean23 conference. As a result of our efforts, the European Marine Board recognised the EMB ECOP Network as a formal part of EMB. 

# Doctorate
In my doctoral thesis, I studied salinity adaptation in the copepod *Acartia tonsa*. Under the supervision of Dr. Reid Brennan, I integrated seascape genomics, transcriptomics, and physiology to understand how copepods adapt along the salinity gradient of the Baltic Sea and predict the species' resilience to future changes. My research included field work in combination with scientific cruises, lab experiments, and bioinformatic approaches.

Early in my project, I had the chance to participate in a two-week workshop on Genomics in Czesky Krumlov which allowed me to get familiar with genomic theories and workflows. I participated in multiple international conferences to connect with colleagues and presented my research to the European Society for Evolutionary Biology (ESEB), the European Marine Biology Symposium, and the Society for Experimental Biology (SEB).

# Science Communication
Science communication has been part of my professional journey since the beginning. My voluntary service and the following job as student assistant taught me the importance of communicating scientific contents to the public and the effect it can have. Over the years, I found more way to integrate science communication in my work and I started to develop art as a tool to effectively convey knowledge and spark interest. During my PhD, I talked about my life as a doctoral researcher and my study organim on various stages and even got to develop a childrens coloring book in collaboration with GEOMAR's Communications & Media Team. In addition, I completed a certificate in Science Communication at the Graduate Center of Kiel University.
